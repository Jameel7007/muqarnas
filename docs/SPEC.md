A scroll-driven 3D rendering of the stalactite vault, generated from its plan.
Successor to *From the Point*.

Primary sources:
- Ghiyāth al-Dīn al-Kāshī, *Miftāḥ al-Ḥisāb* (Key to Arithmetic, 1427), Book IV, ch. 9 — the oldest known definition and measurement of the muqarnas.
- Y. Dold-Samplonius, "Practical Arabic Mathematics: Measuring the Muqarnas by al-Kāshī," *Centaurus* 35 (1992), 193–242.
- Y. Dold-Samplonius & S. Harmsen, "The Muqarnas Plate Found at Takht-i Sulaymān: A New Interpretation," *Muqarnas* 22 (2005), 85–94.
- G. Necipoğlu, *The Topkapı Scroll: Geometry and Ornament in Islamic Architecture* (1995), with al-Asad's essay on muqarnas geometry.

## 0. Governing principles

1. **The geometry is constructed; the arrangement is read.** No vertex is traced: every element
   outline is generated by the library from the alphabet, in exact arithmetic. The Takht-i
   Sulaymān plate's *arrangement* — which element sits where, at what orientation — is digitized
   from the vector line work of Harmsen's fig. 5.17(a) by `tools/digitize-plate.mjs`, regularized
   onto the lattice, and stored as kind + isometry, never as vertices, so the library still
   constructs every outline itself.
2. **All measurements derive from one module.** Al-Kāshī's *miqyās*, the base of the largest facet. Every rib, every tier height, the profile curve, all of it.
3. **The viewer must see the construction happen.** The vault is never presented finished. It is drawn flat, then lifted.
4. **The scan stays local.** No source PDF in the repo, ever.
5. **Light is the subject.** The cells are concave, and what a muqarnas *is* is the behaviour of light in a stepped concavity. Lighting is not polish applied at the end, it is the deliverable.

---

## 1. Why this is the harder project, stated honestly

The plan does not determine the vault. Two muqarnas with identical plane projections can differ locally, by a handful of exchanged elements, or globally, in overall shape and in number of tiers. Reconstruction is constrained but not resolved by the plan; you also need the proportions of the space the vault must fill.

This means the project cannot be "implement the algorithm." There is no single algorithm. There is a generative system with a solution space, and the site's real subject is that space.

Accept this early rather than discovering it in month two. It makes the build harder, because correctness is a range rather than a value and the tests have to check validity instead of equality. It also makes the site original: nobody has made a scroll piece whose climax is *the same plan resolving two different ways*.

## 2. Architecture

```
/packages
  /plan       pure TS. The 2D element alphabet, tiling, and plan generation. No 3D.
  /lift       pure TS. Plan + tier assignment → watertight 3D mesh. No three imports.
  /render     three/webgpu + TSL. Lighting, materials, camera.
  /scenes     GSAP ScrollTrigger choreography.
```

`plan` is genuinely reusable from *From the Point* in spirit and possibly in code — it is 2D constructive geometry on a 45° grid.

The `plan` / `lift` split is the important one. It mirrors the historical division of labour: the master drew the *ṭarḥ*, the builder raised it. Keeping them separate is what makes scene 7 possible, because it lets you feed one plan to two different lift configurations.

### Stack

| Concern | Choice | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Build | Vite + TypeScript | same as *From the Point* |
| Renderer | `three/webgpu` with TSL | WebGPU has been production-ready in three.js since r171, with automatic WebGL2 fallback; TSL is now the primary shader authoring path, so write it from day one rather than migrating later |
| Scroll | GSAP ScrollTrigger, pinned + scrubbed | reuse the machinery that already works for you |
| Smoothing | Lenis | unchanged |
| Camera authoring | Theatre.js, dev only | timeline editor rather than guessing coordinates; export JSON and drop the studio from the production bundle |
| Tests | Vitest | property and invariant tests, not snapshots |

## 3. The plan language

### 3.1 The alphabet

All elements derive from two seeds at unit rib length: the **square** and the **rhombus** with angles 45°/135°. Everything else is a division or a complement.

| Element | Derivation | Role |
|---|---|---|
| square (*murabbaʿ*) | seed | cell or intermediate |
| half-square | square bisected on the diagonal | cell |
| rhombus (*muʿayyan*) | seed, 45°/135° | cell or intermediate, multiple orientations |
| half-rhombus | rhombus bisected | cell |
| jug (*barmak*) | quarter of a regular octagon; two unit ribs, short diagonal of unit length | cell only |
| large biped | square minus jug | intermediate only, usually paired with a jug |
| almond (*bādām*) | deltoid; two curved unit sides meeting at 45°, opposite angle 135°, two right angles | cell |
| small biped | rhombus minus almond | intermediate |
| barley kernel | narrow lens | occasional |

Nine shapes. Generate all nine from the square and rhombus by construction; do not hardcode their vertices. Angles are multiples of 45° throughout, with the isosceles triangles along the diagonal as the standard exception.

Note the cell / intermediate distinction from al-Kāshī: **cells** are the main structural units with a facet and a roof, **intermediate elements** connect adjacent cells and have no roof of their own. The type system should enforce this — a jug can never be an intermediate, a large biped can never be a cell.

```ts
type Role = 'cell' | 'intermediate';
interface PlanElement {
  kind: ElementKind;
  role: Role;
  transform: Mat3;      // placement on the 45° grid
  outline: PlanCurve[]; // straight ribs and arcs, unit-derived
}
```

### 3.2 Plan assembly

A plan is a non-overlapping tiling of a sector — typically an eighth or a quarter — with the vault's centre at one corner and reflection along the diagonal. The Takht-i Sulaymān plate is a quarter-vault plan of exactly this kind, and it is the canonical worked example to validate against.

```ts
function assemblePlan(sector: Sector, seed: PlanSpec): Plan
function reflectToFull(plan: Plan): Plan
```

**Hard invariant:** elements tile the sector with no overlap and no gap. This is not an aesthetic preference, it is what makes the projection lossless and therefore what makes the whole method work.

## 4. The profile curve

Al-Kāshī's construction, which the library must reproduce as a construction rather than as a spline fit:

1. From the upper line, strike an oblique at **30°** intersecting the opposite vertical.
2. Divide the oblique into **five equal parts**.
3. Rotate **two-fifths** down to meet the vertical.
4. The vertical distance from the base to where the curve begins is the **factor**, and it is the value that drives surface computation.

```ts
function kashiProfile(module: number): Curve
function factorOf(profile: Curve): number
```

The design of the curves on the Takht-i Sulaymān elements follows approximately this same construction, which is the historical warrant for using it as the default across the whole vault.

## 5. Tiers and lifting

### 5.1 Structure

A muqarnas is two or more **tiers**, each a horizontal layer of adjacent elements. Within a tier, all facets share a common bottom line and all roofs share a common top line, both parallel to the horizon. Tier height is constant and module-derived.

```ts
interface Tier {
  index: number;
  elements: PlacedElement[];
  baseZ: number;        // index * tierHeight
}
function lift(plan: Plan, assignment: TierAssignment): Vault
```

### 5.2 The tier assignment problem

`TierAssignment` maps each plan element to a tier index. **This is the ambiguity.** The plan constrains it — adjacency and support rules mean not every assignment is buildable — but it does not fix it.

Rules the solver must enforce:

- every cell rests on the roof of a cell below, or on the springing course
- adjacent elements in the same tier share a full rib
- intermediates sit between cells in the same tier, never alone
- the vault must close: the topmost tier resolves to a single crowning element or a small closing group
- total rise must fit the target proportion of the space

```ts
function enumerateAssignments(plan: Plan, constraints: VaultConstraints): TierAssignment[]
```

Return *all* valid assignments, not the first. Scene 7 needs at least two.

### 5.3 Recursive stacking

For the Anatolian Seljuk family, layers stack recursively and the symmetry axis branches hierarchically as it rises — the pattern at tier *n+1* is a transformed descendant of tier *n* rather than an independent design. Implement this as an optional generator mode alongside hand-specified plans; it is what lets the site generate a *new* muqarnas at the end rather than only replaying historical ones.

## 6. The four types

Al-Kāshī's classification, which doubles as the build order:

1. **Simple** (*sāda*) — plane facets, plane roofs. Build first. All geometry is flat polygons; no curve pipeline needed.
2. **Clay-plastered** (*muṭayyan*) — also plane facets and roofs, distinguished by its tier heights. Cheap once type 1 exists.
3. **Curved** (*qawsī*) — curved facet and roof surfaces, using the §4 profile. This is the visually canonical type and the one most Mamluk portals belong to.
4. **Shīrāzī** — pentagons, hexagons, octagons, and multi-pointed stars in the plan. This breaks the 45° grid and needs a generalised plan engine. Treat as a stretch goal, not v1.

Ship types 1 and 3. Mention 4 in the about section; do not promise it.

## 7. Test list

| Test | Assertion |
|---|---|
| **Projection identity** | project the generated 3D vault orthographically down; the result must equal the input plan exactly |
| Tiling closure | plan elements cover the sector with zero overlap and zero gap |
| Alphabet derivation | all nine elements reproduce from square + rhombus by construction, with correct angles |
| Role enforcement | no jug as intermediate, no large biped as cell |
| Tier continuity | tier *n* upper polyline equals tier *n+1* lower polyline |
| Support | every cell has support beneath it; no floating elements |
| Watertightness | intrados mesh is manifold, no holes, consistent normals |
| Closure | topmost tier resolves; the vault has a crown |
| **al-Kāshī oracle** | computed surface area of a generated vault matches al-Kāshī's method using the module and factor, within tolerance |
| Takht-i Sulaymān | the quarter-plan reproduces from the alphabet; at least one valid lift fits a plausible vault proportion |

The projection identity test is the spine of the project — it is the formal statement of "the plan is a lossless top view," and if it passes, the lift is honest.

The al-Kāshī oracle is the one to be proud of. A fifteenth-century surface-area method validating a WebGPU mesh is a genuinely good test, and it is the sort of thing that belongs in the about section.

## 8. Scene arc

**1 — The square and the circle.** A square room. A round dome. They do not meet. The zone of transition stated as a problem before any ornament appears. This is the argument the whole vault answers.

**2 — The module.** One length, the *miqyās*, defined as the base of the largest facet. Then al-Kāshī's profile drawn live: the 30° oblique, the five divisions, the two-fifths rotated down, the factor measured. Still entirely 2D, still in the visual language of *From the Point*.

**3 — The alphabet.** Square and rhombus. The square divides into jug and large biped; the rhombus into almond and small biped; the halves appear. Nine shapes from two, each drawn from its construction.

**4 — The plan.** The elements assemble into a quarter plan on the 45° grid, then reflect to the full plan. The screen now holds a flat drawing that looks like a rosette — and the caption says it is not a rosette, it is a building seen from above.

**5 — The lift.** The camera leaves the plane for the first time. Each plan tile rises into a cell along the §4 profile. First tier only. This is the hinge moment of the site and should be given room.

**6 — Tier on tier.** The vault builds upward and inward, tier by tier, corbelling toward the crown, until it closes.

**7 — The same plan, twice.** The vault descends back into its plan. The plan holds. It rises again — differently. Same projection, different tier assignment, different vault. Both correct. The caption carries the real content: the drawing does not determine the building, and the master's knowledge was never fully in the plan.

**8 — From below.** The camera drops to where a person actually stands, and the vault is seen from underneath as it was built to be seen. Light rakes across the cells. Nothing moves except the light.

**9 — The return.** The vault dissolves down through its tiers, into the plan, into the module, into the point.

## 9. Rendering notes

**This is a lighting project.** Stepped concave cells are an ambient-occlusion problem before they are a shading problem. Budget for it: screen-space AO will look muddy at these depths, so consider baked AO per tier, or a light-probe volume, or an offline pass whose result you ship.

**Camera.** The default view of a muqarnas is from below and close. Resist the museum-object orbit. Scene 8 exists precisely to correct for the fact that scenes 5–7 must cheat the camera outward for legibility.

**Material.** Plaster, not metal. No emissive gold. The historical material is carved or cast plaster and its whole quality is diffuse light in shallow shadow. A physically plausible rough dielectric will read as more serious than anything with a bloom pass.

**Geometry.** Cells are extrusions along the profile curve with roof surfaces. Generate them as parametric patches, not booleans — no CSG anywhere in this project. Booleans on curved surfaces at this cell count will produce non-manifold garbage and destroy the watertightness test.

## 10. Build order

1. `plan` package: alphabet from square and rhombus, tiling, projection invariants. Renders as flat SVG for inspection. Ship when Takht-i Sulaymān's quarter plan reproduces.
2. `kashiProfile` and the factor, tested against the surface-area oracle for a single cell.
3. `lift` for a **simple** (plane-faceted) vault, two tiers. Watertight. No curves yet.
4. Curved type, full profile.
5. Debug page: plan on the left, live vault on the right, a tier-assignment picker between them. **This page is the project.** If it feels good here, the rest is choreography — and this page *is* scene 7 already.
6. Lighting language, locked before scene work.
7. Scenes 5, 7, 1 in that order — the hinge, the climax, then the opening.
8. Remaining scenes, then the return.
9. Typography, captions, loader, about.

---

## 11. What makes this succeed or fail

It succeeds if the viewer understands, by scene 7, that they are looking at a system rather than an object — that the plan is a language and the vault is one sentence in it.

It fails in two identifiable ways. The first is a lighting failure: stepped concavities rendered flatly look like folded paper, and no amount of geometric rigour survives that. The second is a legibility failure: if scenes 5 and 6 pull the camera too far out for clarity, the piece becomes a museum object rotating in a void, which is the opposite of what a muqarnas is. Scene 8 exists to correct for that, and it should be built early enough to prove the correction works.
