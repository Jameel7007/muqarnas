I — the square and the circle

A square room. A round dome.

I

They do not meet. Whatever the dome stands on, the corners are left over.

I

The zone of transition — the gap between the room a builder can make and the dome a builder wants. Everything that follows is its answer.

II — the module

One measure runs through everything that follows: the miqyās, the base of the largest facet. A cell stands exactly two of it tall.

II

The masons' recipe, as al-Kāshī wrote it down: strike an oblique at thirty degrees, divide it in five, fold two fifths onto the vertical. A facet, one sixth of a circle, a ramp — nothing else.

The taʿdīl: so much surface stands over every module of plan — the facet counts whole, the roof tapers to its apex and counts half. Al-Kāshī tabled 1;43,33,45,41, a breath short in the third place: the slip hides in his facet digits.

Two of them, face to face: the niche every cell is cast from. The whole alphabet wears this one curve.

This is not a rosette. It is a building, seen from above.

V — the lift

The first tier stands. Here it begins in the corners — every valid reading of the plate starts there, two cells to a corner.

VII — the same plan, twice

A vault stands: one reading of the drawing, seventeen tiers to the crown ring.

VII

The vault returns to its plan. The plan holds.

VII

It rises again — differently. Same projection, different building, both correct. The drawing does not determine the building, and the master's knowledge was never fully in the plan.
