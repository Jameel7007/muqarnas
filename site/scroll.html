I — the square and the circle

A square room. A round dome.

I

They do not meet. Whatever the dome stands on, the corners are left over.

I

The zone of transition — the gap between the room a builder can make and the dome a builder wants. Everything that follows is its answer.

II — the module

One measure runs through everything that follows: the miqyās, the base of the largest facet. A cell stands exactly two of it tall.

II

The masons' recipe, as al-Kāshī wrote it down: strike an oblique at thirty degrees, divide it in five, fold two fifths onto the vertical. A facet, one sixth of a circle, a ramp — nothing else.

II 0;00,00,00,00

The taʿdīl: so much surface stands over every module of plan — the facet counts whole, the roof tapers to its apex and counts half. Al-Kāshī tabled 1;43,33,45,41, a breath short in the third place: the slip hides in his facet digits.

II

Two of them, face to face: the niche every cell is cast from. The whole alphabet wears this one curve.

III — the alphabet

Two seeds, one rib: the square, and the rhombus of its half-turn — forty-five degrees and its complement. Everything else is a division or a remainder.

III

Bisect each seed for its halves. Cut a quarter octagon from the square's corner: the jug, and the large biped it leaves behind. Drop perpendiculars inside the rhombus: the almond, and the small biped around it.

III

Eight letters. Six know their apex — the marked corner, its two module sides the curved ones, where the profile rises. The seeds decide theirs only when placed. Cells wear roofs; bipeds only connect.

III

Letters into words: the plate's own corner, where every reading begins. The full drawing spells the sky with — letters, yet uses only four of the eight: — squares, — rhombi, — half-rhombi, and — jugs paired on the diagonals. No almond, no biped — a word never needs the whole alphabet. That word is next.

IV — the plan

The drawing board again, and the same square field. The master rules the frame, the seams, and the diagonals — and begins in a corner.

IV

One quarter is enough to draw: — letters, written from the corner in toward the crown — the same order the vault will be built in, because the outermost ring is the lowest tier.

IV

Symmetry does the rest, a right angle at a time. This is the medium's own economy: the Takht-i Sulaymān plate records only a quarter — the turning was understood.

IV

Four diamonds stitch the seams no quarter can hold, and the word is whole: — letters. Now it stops being a drawing.

IV → V

This is not a rosette. It is a building, seen from above.

V — the lift

The first tier stands. Here it begins in the corners — every valid reading of the plate starts there, two cells to a corner.

VI — tier on tier

Each ring stands on the roofs of the ring below. Nothing floats: a cell must land where the tier beneath will carry it, or the whole reading is refused.

VI

Halfway up, the geometry begins doing what it is for: the cells cup the low light, and the vault carves itself into shadow and blade.

VI

The crown closes to a ring, not a point — the plan was an annulus all along, — tiers to its rim. Stand beneath it: everything the drawing knew is over your head.

VII — the same plan, twice

A vault stands: one reading of the drawing, seventeen tiers to the crown ring.

VII

The vault returns to its plan. The plan holds.

VII

It rises again — differently. Same projection, different building, both correct. The drawing does not determine the building, and the master's knowledge was never fully in the plan.

VIII — the day

Nothing will move now except the sun. Watch what the geometry was for.

VIII

Each cell is a cup set at its own angle. As the key walks the horizon, every cup fills and empties in its own time — the same plaster, read differently by the hour.

VIII

The sun comes back to where it began. It was the only thing that moved, and nothing looked the same twice.

IX — the return

The ember hour. Ring gives back ring, the way it was raised.

IX

Down through the tiers, into the plan. The light goes out of the plaster, and the ink — which never needed it — comes back through the dark.

IX

Into the module. Into the point it was struck from. Everything here grew out of this, and goes back into it. Good night.
