Muqarnas

the vault from its plan

A muqarnas is the stalactite vault of Islamic architecture: tiers of small carved cells, corbelled ring over ring, that turn a dome's underside into stepped concavities of light. This piece raises one — the vault implied by a fourteenth-century construction drawing — and lets you watch every step of the raising, because the subject of the piece is precisely that the raising is not in the drawing.

Nothing is traced

No scan was digitised into vertices. The plan is generated from an alphabet of eight elements — two seeds, the square and the 45° rhombus, and their divisions and remainders — placed on the lattice ℤ + ℤ·(√2/2) and computed in exact arithmetic over ℚ(√2), so the tiling closes with zero tolerance rather than within one. Every measurement descends from one module, al-Kāshī's miqyās: the profile curve, the tier heights, the coefficient the plaster is billed in — all of it is that one length, repeated with knowledge.

The drawing

The canonical example is the plate found in the Mongol-era scroll from Takht-i Sulaymān: a quarter-vault plan of 157 elements, published and read in Muqarnas 22. Reflected and turned, it spells the full sky with 632 letters — using only four of the eight kinds. The digitisation here surfaced a quiet fact: the printed plate stretches one band of its field by exactly 5 − 3.5√2 modules to fit its frame. The lattice version needs no such kindness; the regular field span is 7 + 3.5√2.

The method

The profile every cell wears is al-Kāshī's "method of the masons" (Miftāḥ al-Ḥisāb, IV.9): strike a 30° oblique, divide it in five, fold two fifths onto the vertical — which forces the facet height 2 − (3/5)√3, nobody chose it — then one sixth of a circle of radius four fifths, and a ramp. Measured whole-and-half along its length the profile bills 1;43,33,48,40 modules of surface per module of plan; al-Kāshī's table prints 1;43,33,45,41, a breath short in the third sexagesimal place, and the slip hides in his facet digits — Dold-Samplonius found it five centuries later. The piece shows both numbers, because the record's one flaw is also part of the record.

The ambiguity

The plan does not determine the vault. A solver enforces the buildability rules — every ring standing on the roofs of the ring below, heights propagating without divergence — and returns all valid readings, not the first. For this plate every reading begins in the corners, two cells to a corner; one reading reaches seventeen tiers to the crown ring, another eighteen. The climax of the piece descends one building into the flat drawing and raises a different building out of it, because at height zero both are the drawing. The master's knowledge was never fully in the plan.

The light and the colour

A muqarnas hangs over an opening, so light never falls on it from the sky: it bounces up warm from the court and rakes in low through the opening. The whole lighting of the piece is three fixed states of that language, and its eighth scene moves nothing but the sun. The cells' curved canopy wears a fired turquoise wash that sinks toward cobalt in its depths — Takht-i Sulaymān's cells wore glazed colour, and the glaze here is colour and gloss only: no metal, no glow that the light did not put there.
